# Mass balance: can conservation refute a reported zero?

Generated by `scripts/20_mass_balance.py`.

Every other argument in this paper is epistemic, and a reviewer may answer that treating a non-detection as an interval is a modelling convention. This test is not a convention. Where an analyte is measured upstream and reported as `0.0` downstream, zero-substitution asserts that the entire load was removed in between. Conservation of mass lets that assertion be checked against the largest load the downstream non-detection actually permits, $L^{\max}_{\mathrm{down}} = \mathrm{LOD}\times Q_{\mathrm{down}}$.

> Of the **103** disappearances that can be decided, **43 (41.7%)** are fully explained by dilution. For these, treating the downstream zero as an absence asserts a removal that the data do not support and that mass balance does not require. This is a physical result, independent of how one prefers to model censoring.

### Flow duplicated between campaigns

- `C1` (Aug-2017) and `C2` (Nov-2017) carry **identical flow at all 71 stations**. Two different seasons cannot share a discharge record station for station; one campaign's gauging is missing and was filled from the other.

Loads for those campaigns are therefore not independent. Restricting to the campaigns with their own gauging leaves **46** decidable comparisons, of which **24 (52.2%)** are explained by dilution alone -- slightly *more* than the figure over all campaigns, so the duplication is not what produces the result.

### A data-quality finding that had to be handled first

In **60** comparisons the recorded flow *decreases* downstream, which a river does not do. The gaugings are instantaneous and taken on different days, so these are measurement artefacts. They matter because the error is not symmetric: an understated $Q_{\mathrm{down}}$ shrinks $L^{\max}_{\mathrm{down}}$ and pushes the comparison towards *attenuation required*. Every one of them did exactly that, and none landed in the other class. Counting them would have biased the result **against** the hypothesis this script tests, so they are set aside and reported here instead.

Flow increases across these reaches by a median factor of **2.00** (p10 1.14, p90 4.75), which is the mechanism: a conserved load spread through more water falls below a limit that was adequate upstream.

## Mass attributed to removal

- removal implied by reading every downstream zero as an absence: **2.8 kg/day** summed over reach x analyte x campaign
- removal that conservation actually requires: **1.9 kg/day**
- **32.6%** of the apparent removal is an artefact of the reporting convention

These are loads at instantaneous sampling flows summed over comparisons, not a basin budget; they measure how much inferred attenuation rests on substituted zeros.

## What this does and does not show

- Flow is instantaneous at sampling, not a gauged mean.
- Travel time is ignored: the two samples are treated as one parcel of water, which four seasonal snapshots cannot establish.
- Unmeasured lateral inflow dilutes further, so the `masked_by_dilution` count is conservative.

The test therefore counts how often a reported zero **cannot** be read as removal. It does not estimate attenuation rates, and no such estimate is offered.
