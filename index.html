What it is

CENSO extends SOSA/SSN for measurements produced by a method that has a limit of detection. Its modelling commitment is that the limit is carried onto the result. SSN-System binds a detection limit to a sensor; CHMO, the closest prior art, binds it to the method, as a figure of merit of an assay. Neither can say that a particular measurement fell below it — and “censored” is a property of a result, not of the procedure that produced it.

Because the limit reaches the result, a non-detection is typed as the interval [0, LOQ] it actually establishes rather than stored as a zero, and compliance is three-valued: compliant, exceeding, or not determinable — with the third subtyped by the reason it could not be decided.

The third outcome is not a convenience:

Where a result is referred to as “less than limit of quantification”, and the limit of quantification of that technique is above the EQS, the result for the substance being measured shall not be considered for the purposes of assessing the overall chemical status of that water body.
— Directive 2008/105/EC, Article 3(3b)

That outcome is neither compliant nor exceeding. A two-valued data model cannot express it, so compliance with the provision cannot be audited.
