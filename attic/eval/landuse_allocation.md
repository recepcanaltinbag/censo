# Land-use allocation and reach construction

Generated by `scripts/04_landuse_allocation.py`.

## What the area fields mean

Verified against the data, because the field names are misleading:

| field | naive reading | what it actually is | evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| `Area_km2` | segment catchment | **watershed attribute**, repeated on every segment of its `WtrID` | 35 watersheds, one distinct value each; naive sum over segments = 804,742 km² |
| `CArea_km2` | cumulative catchment | **local incremental catchment** of the segment's own `CatchID` | 989 catchments for 989 segments; sum = 10,967.2 km² |

Both partitions close on the same basin area (10,967.2 km² by watershed, 10,967.2 km² by catchment), which is what makes the reading above verifiable rather than plausible.

Telescoping self-test: single outlet `S-976`, 989 segments upstream, cumulative catchment 10,967.2 km2 vs basin total 10,967.2 km2.

Cumulative catchment is therefore computed as the sum of `CArea_km2` over a segment's upstream set. `Area_km2` is carried as a descriptive field and never summed.

## Allocation radius

Pressure layers are distributed on provincial extents that reach well beyond the catchment, so allocation must be clipped. The radius is not chosen by eye: it is solved so that the area within *D* of the channel network equals the basin area the network itself reports.

- basin area (Σ`CArea_km2`): **10,967.2 km²**
- channel length: 4,381 km
- solved radius **D = 2,496 m**, covering 10,967 km² on a 250 m lattice
- areal pressures are subdivided on a 200 m lattice, so a polygon spanning several reaches is split between them

## Allocation quality

The proximity partition (nearest segment within *D*) is compared against the network's own local catchments — an independent field in the same dataset:

- Spearman ρ(proximity area, `CArea_km2`) = **0.921**
- mean absolute difference = 2.64 km² per segment (mean catchment 11.09 km²)
- overlap agreement Σmin/Σ`CArea_km2` = **88.1%**

Proximity is therefore a good but imperfect stand-in for the true catchment: it recovers the right ranking of segments and most of the area, and the residual is the price of not having catchment polygons. Reach-level conclusions are drawn from *differences* between nested upstream sets, where this error largely cancels.

- allocation records (feature × segment): **3,129**
- distance to allocated segment: median 566 m, p90 1,775 m, max 2,484 m (bounded by *D*)
