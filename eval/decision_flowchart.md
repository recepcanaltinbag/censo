## Why the order is the argument

**Does the standard apply to what was measured? (bioavailable metal, hardness class)**

First, because it is prior to every other question. If the standard is written over bioavailable lead and the record reports total lead, there is no comparison to make -- not a strict one, not a lenient one.

**Detection status, read from the censoring flag, the value and the LOQ**

Three states from three reported fields, and the LOQ is one of them: a row with no flag AND no limit is unresolved, because nothing bounds what the number means. The flag and the limit are separate statements -- an empty flag says nothing, a '0' says not censored, which is information.

**Is the quantification limit above the standard?  LOQ > T**

Article 3(3b) reaches a result reported as below the quantification limit, and only that. A quantified mean above the standard is evidence of exceedance whatever the method's limit was.

**Is the reported value below its own LOQ?  x < LOQ**

The row contradicts itself. Neither Article 3(3b) nor a comparison applies to a number that cannot be both.

**Is x within T/2 of T — the widest uncertainty the law lets a method have?**

Article 4(1) sets a CEILING, not a test: a method whose results may lawfully be used has an expanded uncertainty of '50 % or below' at the level of the standard. So T/2 is the widest interval the law would permit around a result, and a value inside it is one that a method meeting only the legal minimum could have produced on either side of the standard. The Directive does not instruct this comparison; it is the worst permitted method read against the record, which is the conservative direction and costs 1.0 point of the headline.
