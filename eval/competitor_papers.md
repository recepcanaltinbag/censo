## Conclusion for the gap table

Across five papers, all squarely about ontologies for river water quality, the terms *detection limit*, *LOD*, *LOQ*, *censoring* and *non-detection* do not appear once. All five model regulatory thresholds and three carry a SWRL rule layer, so neither thresholds nor rules are claimed as novel here.

**This must be read together with WHOW.** The Water Health Open Knowledge Graph (`https://w3id.org/whow/onto/water-monitoring`) does publish its ontology, and it *does* represent a below-limit result as a value range -- its own comment gives `<10` meaning `[0, 10]`. WHOW is the closest prior work and this project extends it.

The defensible claim is therefore about the interval's PROVENANCE, not the interval:

> Existing water-quality ontologies either ignore censoring entirely (the five assessed here) or record the resulting interval anonymously (WHOW). None binds the interval to the detection limit that produced it, distinguishes a censored result from a merely imprecise one, or admits an undecidable compliance outcome. Where a detection limit exists at all (SSN's Systems module) it describes the **sensor**, not the **measurement**.
