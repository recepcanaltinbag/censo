# Why an ontology? The argument the paper rests on

Status: draft v0.1 · 2026-08-03

Every ontology paper is rejected by the same reviewer comment:

> *"The authors built a vocabulary and ran a reasoner that inferred nothing surprising.
> This could have been done with a relational schema and SQL. What is the scientific
> contribution?"*

This document is the answer, and it must survive hostile reading. It also states honestly
where the argument is weak.

---

## 1. The core argument: censored data **is** open-world data

### The mismatch
A relational database operates under the **Closed World Assumption** (CWA): what is not asserted
is false. A `0.0` in a concentration column asserts *"the concentration is zero."*

A non-detection asserts something categorically different:

> *We do not know whether this substance is present. We know only that if it is present, its
> concentration lies below the limit of detection.*

That is the **Open World Assumption** (OWA), exactly. Absence of evidence is explicitly not
evidence of absence.

**Therefore: storing a non-detect as `0.0` is not a rounding convention. It is a type error —
open-world data forced into a closed-world model.** The information destroyed is not the
magnitude; it is the *modality*.

### The empirical hook
In this dataset the damage is measurable: **76.9% of chemical measurements — 85.2% of
micropollutants — are stored as exactly `0.0`** (`eval/gate_check.md`). Every downstream mean,
load, trend and compliance statement computed from that table inherits a bias of unknown size,
and no amount of statistics applied afterwards can recover the lost modality.

### Why this is publishable at the CS ∩ environmental science intersection
It is simultaneously:
- **a computer-science claim** — a semantic mismatch between a data model's logical foundation and
  the epistemics of the measurement process, with a formal remedy;
- **an environmental-science claim** — a quantified, reproducible defect in how a real regulatory
  monitoring programme records and reports its results.

Neither community can make this claim alone. That is precisely the intersection the paper needs.

### The regulatory consequence
EU WFD / Turkish ÇKS compliance assessment implicitly requires three verdicts, but the data models
in use can express only two:

| Verdict | Expressible under CWA? | Expressible in OWL |
|---|---|---|
| Compliant | yes | `EQSCompliant` |
| Exceeding | yes | `EQSExceedance` |
| **Cannot be determined** | **no** | `IndeterminateCompliance` |

And the third case is not hypothetical: **14 analytes here have an LOQ above their annual-average
EQS** — Azinphos-methyl by a factor of 115. For those substances every "compliant" record in the
national dataset is an artefact of the analytical method. A closed-world model cannot even
represent the distinction, so the artefact is invisible.

## 2. The second argument: `conc = 0` conflates four different facts

For the detection-onset method this is not philosophy, it is a concrete failure mode.

`WHERE concentration = 0` in SQL merges:

1. measured, genuinely absent;
2. measured, present but below LOD;
3. **never measured** — the analyte was not in that campaign's panel;
4. measured, but the value was lost in transcription.

**All four occur in this dataset.** Case 3 is the dangerous one: if a substance is absent from the
upstream station's panel but present in the downstream station's, the naive query reports a
detection onset where there is only a change of analytical scope. The method would manufacture
sources.

The knowledge graph separates them structurally:

| Situation | Representation |
|---|---|
| measured, quantified | `sosa:Observation` ⊓ `QuantifiedObservation` |
| measured, below LOQ | `sosa:Observation` ⊓ `EstimatedObservation` |
| measured, below LOD | `sosa:Observation` ⊓ `CensoredObservation` |
| never measured | **no observation individual exists** |

SHACL then *validates* which case applies, so onsets arising from panel changes are rejected before
inference rather than reported as findings. **This is a specific, demonstrable error that the
ontology prevents and a spreadsheet workflow does not.** It belongs in the paper as a worked example
with the count of onsets it removes.

## 3. What is genuinely new relative to existing water ontologies

> **VERIFIED POSITION (2026-08-03) — 14 ontologies checked from their own files.**
>
> `eval/gap_table.md`, generated by `scripts/07_verify_gap_table.py`, now covers SOSA, SSN,
> SSN-system, GeoSPARQL, QUDT, SAREF, SAREF4WATR, WHOW, DoCE, InWaterSense (core / regulations /
> pollutants), WAM-ONTO and SewerNet — every one parsed from its published file, not from a paper.
>
> **One pattern runs through all of them: the field describes the instrument, not the measurement.**
>
> | ontology | has a range/limit concept | what it is attached to |
> |---|---|---|
> | SSN-system | `DetectionLimit` | **the sensor** (`SystemProperty`) |
> | InWaterSense core | `hasRangeMinValue` / `hasRangeMaxValue` | **the sensor** (inherited from the SSN *meteo* extension: measurement capability) |
> | SewerNet, SAREF4WATR | `Interval` | **time** (OWL-Time) — a false positive that an earlier draft of the checker scored as a value range |
> | **WHOW** | `Range` + `lowerBound` / `upperBound` | **the result** — the only one |
> | **CENSO** | `resultLowerBound` / `resultUpperBound` + LOD/LOQ on the method | **the result, with its cause** |
>
> So the claim is not that censoring is unrepresented — WHOW represents it. It is that
> **the interval is everywhere anonymous or misattached**: bound to the instrument's capability
> rather than to what a particular measurement establishes, and where bound to a result (WHOW),
> carrying no record of *why* it is an interval.

> **CORRECTION 2 (2026-08-03) — WHOW already represents censored results as intervals.**
>
> The Water Health Open Knowledge Graph publishes its ontology at
> `https://w3id.org/whow/onto/water-monitoring` with content negotiation, so unlike WaWO+ and OPO
> it can be checked directly. It was checked, and it defines a `Range` class with `lowerBound` /
> `upperBound`, attached to `ObservationValue`. Its own comment reads:
>
> > *"The class of value intervals. Examples: the value of the observation of the concentration of
> > copper is `<10`, meaning between 0 and 10."*
>
> **That is censoring, represented as an interval.** The earlier claim that no water ontology
> represents interval-valued results arising from censoring is therefore **false and is withdrawn.**
> WHOW is the true state of the art here, and this work extends it rather than filling a void.
>
> What WHOW does **not** have, verified by inspecting its 31 classes, 20 object properties and
> 4 datatype properties:
>
> | | WHOW | CENSO |
> |---|---|---|
> | interval-valued result | **yes** (`Range`, bounds) | yes |
> | detection limit as a first-class entity | — | yes |
> | interval linked to the limit that produced it | — | yes |
> | censored vs. merely imprecise distinguishable | — | yes (4 disjoint statuses) |
> | compliance vocabulary at all | — | yes |
> | undecidable outcome | — | yes |
> | threshold applicability conditions | — | yes |
>
> **The WHOW range is anonymous.** Nothing records *why* the interval exists — censoring, or
> measurement spread, or a reporting convention — nothing ties it to the LOD of the method that
> produced it, and WHOW has no compliance vocabulary, so "undecidable" cannot even be posed.
>
> The contribution is therefore **not the interval but its provenance and epistemic status**: which
> limit produced it, whether the analyte was detected, and what follows for compliance. State it that
> way in the paper, and cite WHOW as the closest prior work rather than as an absence.

> **CORRECTION 1 (2026-08-03), from `scripts/07_verify_gap_table.py`.**
> An earlier draft of this document claimed that *"SOSA/SSN has no concept of a
> detection limit."* **That claim is false and is withdrawn.** The W3C SSN
> Systems module defines `ssn-system:DetectionLimit`, verified by parsing the
> ontology file itself.
>
> What survives is narrower and sharper, and it is now verified rather than
> asserted:
>
> `ssn-system:DetectionLimit` is a subclass of `ssn-system:SystemProperty` — it
> describes **what a sensor is capable of**. It is never attached to a
> `sosa:Observation` or to its result. Nothing in SOSA/SSN can therefore state
> that *a particular measurement fell below the limit*.
>
> **The limit is known about the instrument and forgotten about the measurement.**
>
> The discriminating question for the gap table is consequently not *"does the
> term exist"* but *"is it bound to the result?"*. See the `LOD bound to` column
> in [`../eval/gap_table.md`](../eval/gap_table.md): SSN-system binds it to the
> sensor; CENSO binds it to the result.
>
> Thresholds are represented by QUDT and SAREF, so **thresholds are explicitly
> not claimed as novel**. What no comparison ontology has is a censoring status
> on a result, an interval-valued result arising from censoring, or an
> undecidable compliance outcome.

The gap is concrete and a reviewer can check it:

|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| SOSA/SSN | ✗ | ✗ | ✗ | ✗ | ✗ |
| WaWO+ | ✗ | ✗ | ✗ | ✗ | ✗ |
| OPO | ✗ | ✗ | ✗ | partial | ✗ |

> **Verification task (WP1, blocking).** Each ✗ above must be confirmed against the actual

The key technical point: **`sosa:Observation` has `hasSimpleResult` — a point value. SOSA/SSN has no
concept of a detection limit.** Nothing in the standard stack lets you say "this observation
establishes only an upper bound." That is the hole this work fills.

## 4. Why it is a reusable pattern, not a case study

This is what lifts the paper from Q2 to a Q1 attempt.

The censored-observation pattern is **domain-independent**. Any measurement process with a detection
limit has the same structure:

| Domain | Same problem |
|---|---|
| Air quality | trace VOCs, PM speciation below LOD |
| Food safety | pesticide residues, mycotoxins |
| Clinical assays | viral load, biomarkers "not detected" |
| Soil / sediment | persistent organics |
| Occupational exposure | below-LOQ exposure records |

So the deliverable is not "an Ergene ontology" but **an OWA-native extension of SOSA/SSN for
censored observations**, published under a permanent IRI with documentation, plus the reasoning
pattern that turns censoring into a four-valued verdict. The Ergene basin is the validation case,
not the contribution.

Frame the abstract accordingly: *pattern first, basin second.*

## 5. What the reasoner does — and does not — do

Overstating this is the fastest way to lose a semantic-web reviewer. State it plainly in the paper.

| Task | Mechanism | Honest statement |
|---|---|---|
| Substance identity across 5 files | OWL + `skos:exactMatch` → ChEBI | reasoner-supported |
| Upstream closure over the network | transitive `upstreamOf`, GeoSPARQL | reasoner-derived |
| Distinguishing not-measured from not-detected | class membership + SHACL | **structural, the key win** |
| Compliance / onset verdicts | OWL **defined classes** over materialised bounds | reasoner-classified, explainable |
| Arithmetic (loads, ratios, dilution test) | SWRL built-ins / SPARQL / Python | **not** OWL — say so |
| Data-quality constraints | SHACL | closed-world validation, deliberately |

The sentence to use: *"OWL supplies identity, topology, classification and explanation; arithmetic
is materialised by rules and code and then classified by the ontology. We do not claim the reasoner
computes mass balances."*

## 6. Where the story is currently weak — fix or disclose

| # | Weakness | Status / plan |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Only 29 immediate reaches.** Small inferential unit. | Reframe the unit as *reach × substance × campaign* → 29 × 222 × 4 ≈ 25,700 observations. Report reach count honestly as a structural limitation. |
| 2 | **No ground truth** for source attribution. Land-use agreement is corroboration only. | Seek discharge-permit records for a subset. Otherwise state clearly and report agreement, not accuracy. |
| 3 | **Sector-from-company-name is a heuristic** (77.4% coverage). | Report coverage; manual validation of a random sample of 100 firms with inter-rater agreement. |
| 4 | Land-use allocation bugs (see `04-progress.md`). | Must be fixed before any number is quoted. |
| 5 | Only 79 of 254 analytes have LOD/LOQ. | This is itself a finding — the rest are *structurally* undecidable. Report as such. |
| 6 | No biodegradation half-lives. | Persistence tier derived from LogP / Henry / solubility; optionally add EPI Suite BIOWIN predictions from the 222 SMILES. |
| 7 | Baseline ontology is still the Protégé default IRI, no imports, no rules. | WP3 rebuild. |

None of these is fatal. All must be visible in the paper — reviewers punish concealment far harder
than limitation.

## 7. Venue positioning

The paper must read as **method + software + environmental application**, in that order.

| Venue | Q | Fit | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Ecological Informatics** | Q1 | **best** | explicitly the CS ∩ ecology intersection; wants reusable informatics methods with real ecological validation |
| **Environmental Modelling & Software** | Q1 | **best** | explicitly wants "generic frameworks and techniques for multi-disciplinary issues" plus software |
| Journal of Web Semantics / Semantic Web Journal | Q2 | good | if the emphasis shifts to the ontology pattern itself; demands high engineering rigour and FAIR publication |
| ISPRS IJGI | Q2 | good | if the geospatial reasoning is foregrounded |
| Water (MDPI) | Q2 | safe | reliable fallback |
| Science of the Total Environment | Q1 | risky | would want more environmental depth, less CS |
| Water Research | Q1 | poor | would reject the informatics contribution as out of scope |

**Strategy:** write for Ecological Informatics; the same manuscript converts to EM&S with minimal
change, and drops to Water / IJGI without restructuring.

## 8. The abstract's logical skeleton

1. Micropollutant monitoring produces mostly non-detections.
2. Non-detections are **open-world facts** stored in **closed-world systems**; the standard practice
   of substituting zero is a type error, not a rounding convention.
3. We quantify the damage in a real regulatory programme: 85.2% of micropollutant records; and 14
   substances whose LOQ exceeds their EQS, so compliance is undecidable in principle.
4. We present an OWA-native extension of SOSA/SSN in which detection limits are first-class and
   compliance and source-onset verdicts are **OWL defined classes**, including an explicit
   *undecidable* state.
5. Coupled with river topology and land use through GeoSPARQL, the same representation turns
   non-detections into **upper bounds**, which localise sources rather than obscuring them.
6. Applied to four campaigns, 75 stations and 222 micropollutants in the Ergene Basin, the framework
   localises onsets to reaches and infers source types that agree with independent land-use
   geometry in *X*% of cases.
7. The pattern is domain-independent; ontology, knowledge graph and code are published under a
   permanent IRI with a DOI.

Point 6 is the only one still unwritten. **Everything else is already evidenced in this repository.**
