## Why the order is the argument

**Does the record establish a bound? (a flag or a limit, and a limit for a flag)**

First, because nothing else can be asked of a record that establishes no interval. A row with neither a censoring flag nor a limit, or flagged below a limit it does not state, is a defect of the record whatever the standard and its conditions are, and it is reported as that.

**Is a standard defined for this substance here?**

Asked after the bound and before everything else, because it is a fact about the REGULATION and not about the record: a jurisdiction that sets no limit for a substance has not found it compliant. No row in this paper's denominator reaches it -- the population is the rows a European standard covers -- and it arises when the same observations are assessed under a second package, which regulates a different list.

**Metal: is the result on the dissolved fraction? (Annex I Part B point 3)**

The water standards for cadmium, lead, mercury and nickel refer to the dissolved concentration. A whole-water result is not a measurement of the quantity the standard is written on, so no verdict is drawn from it -- not even a pass, which would rest on total >= dissolved, our reasoning and not the Directive's.

**Cadmium: is a hardness reported for the station-year? (footnote 9; GD 38 tier 2)**

The cadmium standard depends on the hardness class of the water. The release reports hardness, or calcium and magnesium, on rows of their own; joined, it selects the class standard the comparison below uses. Without it the applicable standard is unknown.

**Lead, nickel: does the dissolved result pass the bioavailable standard?  (GD 38 tier 1)**

The bioavailable concentration cannot exceed the dissolved one, so a dissolved result that clears the bioavailable standard clears it. The comparison below is made, and only a pass or Article 3(3b) is kept; anything else needs a bioavailability model the record cannot feed.

**Detection status, read from the censoring flag, the value and the LOQ**

A flag set below the limit is censored; an unflagged value is quantified. The flag and the limit are separate statements -- an empty flag says nothing, a '0' says not censored, which is information.

**Is the reported mean below its own LOQ?  x < LOQ**

Directive 2009/90/EC Article 5(2): a calculated mean below the limit of quantification shall be referred to as 'less than limit of quantification'. It is a bounded result the reporter did not flag, and it is assessed as one.

**Is the quantification limit above the standard?  LOQ > T**

Article 3(3b) reaches a result reported as below the quantification limit, and compares the limit with the standard as a point. The band is not applied here: the Directive does not apply it, and a limit inside the band is flagged, not reclassified.

**Where is x against T ± U? (U reported, else 0.5 T)**

Article 4(1) sets a CEILING, not a test: a lawfully usable method has an expanded uncertainty of '50 % or below' at the level of the standard. A reported uncertainty is used where the record carries one; none does here, so U = 0.5 T, the widest the law permits, and a value inside it could have been produced on either side of the standard.
